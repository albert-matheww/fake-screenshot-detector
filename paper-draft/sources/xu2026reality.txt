Bridging the Reality Gap in Tampered Text Detection: A Human-Crafted Real-World Dataset and a Text-Centric Approach
Xu, Guitao, Zhang, Peirong, Jin, Lianwen (2026). IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security. doi:10.1109/tifs.2026.3705334

ABSTRACT (as returned by Semantic Scholar):
Detecting tampered text within images has gained significant importance in verifying digital media authenticity. However, current public datasets are constrained by their dependence on synthetic data and limited tampering scenarios, hindering their effectiveness in real-world scenarios. To address this gap, we introduce a comprehensive and diverse dataset for tampered text detection in real-world scenarios named RWTamper. ‘RW’ denotes ‘real-world’, encompassing six key aspects: 1) four tampering types; 2) three text image types; (3) a wide range of image resolutions; 4) rich data sources; 5) completely manual tampering; and 6) multiple degradation conditions. RWTamper consists of three subsets under different degradation conditions, with each subset containing 9,326 high-quality tampered images accompanied by precise mask annotations. In addition, we propose a text-centric model termed TTFN for tampered text detection. Specifically, we employ a novel similarity-based weighted fusion mechanism to effectively minimize feature redundancy and aggregate multi-view tampering cues. Additionally, we exploit fine-grained OCR information to direct the model’s attention towards enhanced discrimination between tampered and authentic text regions while mitigating background interference. The model is trained by our proposed uncertainty-guided optimization strategy, thereby achieving stronger localization performance. Experimental results demonstrate that TTFN outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods, achieving superior performance across most scenarios. Furthermore, extensive analysis reveals that RWTamper serves as a highly challenging dataset, bringing new vitality and providing more perspectives to real-world tampered text detection. The code and dataset are available at https://github.com/SCUT-DLVCLab/TTFN
