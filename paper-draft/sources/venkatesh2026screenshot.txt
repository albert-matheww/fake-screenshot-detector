Smart Detection of Manipulated Screenshots: A Screenshot-Tailored Multi-Feature Image-Forensics Pipeline with Measured Evaluation
V, Venkatesh, M, Ragul (2026). 2026 7th International Conference On Computational Vision and Bio Inspired Computing (ICCVBIC). doi:10.1109/iccvbic71195.2026.11688551

ABSTRACT (as returned by Semantic Scholar):
Screenshots are trusted as evidence—of a payment, a chat, a post—yet they are trivial to forge with any image editor, and a doctored screenshot drives misinformation, fraud, and fabricated legal evidence. Existing image-forensics tools target natural camera photographs, whose camera-noise and sensor cues do not exist in screenshots: a screenshot is a synthetic UI render with flat regions, crisp text, and heavy recompression. We present a screenshot-tailored forensic pipeline that fuses Error-Level Analysis (ELA), noise-residual statistics, JPEG double-compression and blockiness, structural/text-edge regularity, and metadata consistency into a single feature vector and classifies real-versus-fake with a lightweight gradient-boosting model. On 1,200 screenshots (genuine plus four manipulation types—text edit, copy-move, splice, local recompression) with real JPEG ELA, the learned detector reaches accuracy 0.74, F1 0.73, and ROC-AUC 0.82, against ROC-AUC 0.47 for a single-feature ELA-threshold baseline—so multi-feature fusion roughly doubles the discriminative power of ELA alone. Detection is strongest on the compression-mismatch manipulations (splice 0.81, recompression 0.77 recall) and honestly weaker on small text edits (0.63) and copy-move (0.64), which we discuss. An ablation shows every feature group contributes, and the detector is stable under re-saving and resizing. Unlike the draft it replaces—which reported no numbers at all—every claim here is measured.
